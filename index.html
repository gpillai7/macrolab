Case Study

How a Singapore F&B operator uses MacroLab

Mr Goh runs three zi char restaurants in Singapore. Here is how he uses the weekly Decision Brief to make better calls — without reading a single economics paper.

The Question

"Should I raise my set lunch price from $12 to $14 this month, or wait?"

Signals MacroLab Read
  • ⇧ Palm oil +18% YoY (import cost)
  • ⇧ PWM F&B floor wage rises Q3
  • ⇧ Tourist spend at hawkers +22% vs 2019
  • = Local consumer sentiment stable
  • ~ Competitor pricing up ~$1.50 avg
MacroLab Decision Brief Says

Raise now — conditions are as favourable as they will be this year.

Input costs are rising and will not reverse before Q4. Tourist demand provides cover. Local diners have already absorbed competitor increases. A $2 rise is within the current tolerance band.

What Mr Goh Did

Raised to $13.50 (not full $14 — his call). Monitored for 3 weeks. No material drop in covers. Added $14 weekend dinner set in June as a further test. Planning full $14 weekday lunch by August before Q3 wage floor kicks in.

Methodology

MacroLab's analytical framework is built on eight interconnected layers. Each layer is explained below.

Layer 1 — Global Value Chain (GVC) Signal Engine
MacroLab monitors Singapore's position in global value chains across electronics, precision engineering, chemicals, and food manufacturing. The GVC engine tracks upstream disruption signals — such as Chinese factory output changes, Malaysian component lead times, and Batam industrial capacity — and maps their transmission speed and magnitude to Singapore SME input costs. This allows early warning of cost shocks 4–8 weeks before they appear in CPI data.
Layer 2 — MAS Monetary Policy Transmission
Singapore's monetary policy operates through exchange rate management rather than interest rates. MacroLab models how MAS S$NEER slope adjustments affect import prices, export competitiveness, and borrowing costs for SGD-denominated SME debt. The model incorporates MAS policy reaction functions estimated from historical intervention patterns, allowing scenario analysis around likely policy responses to inflationary or recessionary shocks.
Layer 3 — China Macro Linkage Model
China accounts for approximately 13% of Singapore's total trade and significantly larger shares in specific sectors. MacroLab's China module integrates PMI data, property sector health indicators, consumer confidence indices, and outbound tourism flows to project demand spillovers into Singapore. The model separately tracks the manufacturing supply chain channel (B2B) and the consumer demand channel (B2C tourism and retail).
Layer 4 — Singapore Domestic Policy Lens
Singapore's government actively uses fiscal and regulatory tools that directly affect SME operating costs. MacroLab tracks Progressive Wage Model expansions, GST treatment changes, Enterprise Development Grant eligibility windows, foreign worker quota policies, and CPF rate adjustments. Each policy signal is mapped to its cost impact on relevant sectors with a 3–12 month forward projection.
Layer 5 — Corporate Reality Signals
Macro models anchored only in government data miss the ground-level reality of business decisions. MacroLab supplements official data with corporate earnings signals, business sentiment surveys, trade association member feedback, and commercial property transaction data. These signals are used to calibrate the macro model's sector-specific outputs and to detect divergences between policy assumptions and actual business behaviour.
Layer 6 — Probabilistic Signal Architecture
MacroLab does not produce single-point forecasts. Instead, the signal architecture generates probability distributions across plausible macro regimes, weighted by current signal strength. This allows users to understand not just the most likely outcome, but the range of plausible outcomes and their relative likelihood — a more honest representation of macroeconomic uncertainty than a single number.
Layer 7 — Singapore-Specific Labour Signal Output (SSLO)
Labour costs are the largest operating cost for most Singapore SMEs. The SSLO layer integrates Ministry of Manpower data, NTUC wage trend reports, sector-specific job vacancy indices, foreign worker dependency ratios, and Progressive Wage Model expansion schedules. The output is a forward-looking labour cost pressure index by sector, updated monthly and incorporated into each sector playbook.
Layer 8 — Tipping Point Detection Logic
Many macro regime shifts are non-linear — conditions can appear stable until a threshold is crossed, at which point the system shifts rapidly. MacroLab's tipping point layer monitors composite indicator thresholds that historically precede regime changes: freight rate acceleration, credit spread widening, FX volatility clustering, and consumer confidence cliff edges. When multiple thresholds are simultaneously approached, the system elevates its scenario probability weights accordingly.
